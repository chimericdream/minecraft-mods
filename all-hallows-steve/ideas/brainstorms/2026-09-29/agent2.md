A strong direction would be **harvest season, woodland atmosphere, and handmade autumn traditions**. That gives the mod a distinct Halloween identity through warm light, carved pumpkins, dried plants, and seasonal rituals.

Your carving station is a good centerpiece. I’d build outward from it:

- **Pumpkin lanterns with distinct light patterns.** Placing a candle inside a carved pumpkin illuminates its stencil. Different candle positions or liners could change the appearance of the eyes and openings. Hanging and wall-mounted versions would extend their use around paths and porches.

- **Weathered pumpkins.** Optional aging stages take pumpkins from freshly carved to faded and softly weathered. Waxing preserves a preferred stage; composting gives old pumpkins a useful end. Keep aging slow or opt-in so decorative builds remain easy to maintain.

- **Botanical dyes and finishes.** Give the carving station finishes as well as colors: matte wax, pale whitewash, dark stain, or a subtle metallic accent. These could make the same stencil suit a rustic farm, an elegant manor, or a woodland cottage.

- **Fallen leaves you can gather and arrange.** Thin, stackable leaf layers in muted gold, rust, and brown. A rake collects them for paths, garden beds, compost, or crafting. Occasional drifting leaves would add atmosphere without requiring a full seasons system.

- **Wreaths and garlands.** Combine dried leaves, wheat, twigs, berries, and small gourds into restrained decorations for doors, beams, and fences. A few material combinations could produce distinct designs without needing another elaborate crafting interface.

- **A drying rack.** Hang apples, herbs, corn, and flowers to preserve food or create decorative bundles. It adds a useful process and makes kitchens, barns, and market stalls feel inhabited.

- **Orchard harvesting and a cider press.** Apple baskets, pressing, and fresh cider create a small autumn food loop. Apple pomace could become compost or animal feed, connecting the feature to farming.

- **Heirloom squash and gourds.** A handful of varieties with different silhouettes and subdued colors would make harvest displays much richer. Give each a small purpose—baking, soup, seeds, or durable decoration—so they aren’t just inventory clutter.

- **Woven harvest baskets.** Small containers that visibly display apples, pumpkins, or other produce when filled. They would serve as both useful storage and decoration, especially alongside farms and carving stations.

- **Quiet wind decorations.** Wooden wind chimes, dried seed-pod rattles, or suspended leaves with infrequent, soft sounds. These could give porches and gardens a seasonal soundscape.

- **Field markers and rustic scarecrows.** Straw-and-cloth constructions with customizable clothing and carved pumpkin heads. Keeping them stationary makes them grounded agricultural decorations; they don’t need animations or supernatural behavior.

- **Stencils discovered through exploration.** Add designs inspired by oak leaves, wheat sheaves, moths, owls, and phases of the moon. Different village professions or structures could offer different patterns, making your existing system part of exploration.

For wearable pumpkins, **subtle stencil-specific vision overlays** could be enough to make them feel special. The shape of the carving determines the openings, while an accessibility setting controls how much they obscure vision. They already have a useful vanilla relationship with Endermen, so extra powers aren’t necessary.

My strongest first additions would be **lantern variants, fallen leaves, botanical wreaths, and harvest baskets**. Together, they let players create an entire autumn setting around your carving station, with a manageable amount of new machinery or progression.
