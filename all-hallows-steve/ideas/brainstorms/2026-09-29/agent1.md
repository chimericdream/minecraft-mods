Here are some ideas that stay grounded in the harvest season, craft, and atmosphere rather than spooky or goofy territory. I've grouped them around what you already have, then broader seasonal additions.

**Extending the carving system**

- **Carving byproducts.** Carving a pumpkin at the station could yield pulp and seeds. Seeds can be roasted on a campfire as a snack, and pulp can go into a composter or be used in baking.
- **Lighting inserts.** Instead of the carved pumpkin automatically becoming a lantern, players place a candle, torch, or soul torch inside. Light level and color follow the insert, and candles could inherit the dye color of the candle used.
- **Stencil-dependent light.** More open stencils let out more light, and fine or detailed stencils give a dimmer glow. This gives players a reason to choose a design for function as well as looks.
- **Decay and waxing.** Carved pumpkins slowly soften and wilt over in-game days unless waxed with honeycomb, which mirrors the vanilla copper mechanic. It fits the idea that jack-o'-lanterns are temporary unless you take care of them.
- **Custom stencil editor.** A pixel-grid editor in the station lets players design their own face patterns, saved as stencil items that can be copied or traded. This is probably the biggest single addition to the mod's depth.

**Gourds and harvest crops**

- **Squash and pumpkin varieties.** White pumpkins, warty heirloom pumpkins, butternut and acorn squash, and small ornamental gourds, each with its own texture. Some could be carvable and others decorative or edible only.
- **Giant pumpkins.** A slow-growing crop that expands into a 2×2 or 3×3 block pumpkin when fertilized carefully and given space. It rewards patient farming and works well as a centerpiece.
- **Tall corn.** A crop that grows two to three blocks tall, useful for building field mazes and yielding corn, husks, and stalks. Husks and stalks can be bundled into decorative shocks.
- **Orchards and a cider press.** Apple trees (and possibly pear trees) that bear fruit seasonally, plus a press block that turns apples into cider. Cider could age in barrels into a stronger variant.

**Seasonal atmosphere**

- **Leaf litter.** Deciduous trees shed leaf layers onto the ground over time, colored to match the tree. Players can rake them into piles or compost them, and leaf layers could muffle footstep sounds.
- **Autumn foliage variants.** Placeable red, orange, and gold leaf blocks for builders, obtained from a new maple tree or by shearing leaves during a seasonal window.
- **Maple sap.** Tap maple trees with a spout and bucket to collect sap, then boil it into syrup at a campfire or furnace. It's a nice slow, seasonal activity.
- **Morning frost and low fog.** Light ground fog in forest and plains biomes at dawn, plus frost overlays on grass that melt in sunlight. These are purely ambient but do a lot for mood.

**Home and craft**

- **Harvest decorations.** Wreaths made from leaves, dried flowers, and small gourds; hanging herb bundles; hay-bale seating; and corn-husk garlands. These give players a way to decorate that isn't about monsters or cartoonish props.
- **Drying rack.** A block that slowly dries herbs, flowers, and corn husks for use in decorations and recipes.
- **Knitted clothing.** Dyeable scarves, sweaters, and caps crafted from wool. These could provide light resistance to freezing in cold biomes and would pair naturally with wearable carved pumpkins.
- **Seasonal cooking.** Roasted chestnuts, apple crumble, squash soup, and spiced cider. Warm foods could give a mild comfort effect, such as faster hunger regeneration after coming in from the cold.

**Functional pieces tied to your existing feature**

- **Scarecrows.** A buildable scarecrow that takes a carved pumpkin as its head and can wear armor or knitted clothing. Functionally, it keeps crows or other crop-eating birds off nearby farmland, so it earns its place rather than being pure decor.
- **Harvest moon.** A rare full moon event in autumn where the moon appears larger and warmer in tone, and crops grow faster overnight. It gives the season a sense of occasion without leaning on horror.

If you only pick a few, the lighting inserts, waxing and decay, and scarecrows connect most directly to the carving station and would make carved pumpkins matter beyond decoration.
