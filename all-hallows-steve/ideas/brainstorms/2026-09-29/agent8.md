Here are feature ideas that stay in a grounded autumn register: harvest, craft, weather, and quiet seasonal atmosphere. Nothing costume-party, nothing jump-scare.

**Harvest and food**
- Sap tapping. Copper or iron spiles on maple-like trees in autumn; buckets fill slowly with sap, then reduce in a cauldron or smoker into syrup. Syrup sweetens bread, porridge, or baked apples and can glaze carved pumpkins so the dye reads richer.
- A small preserving set. Glass jars, dried apple rings, pickled gourds, mushroom conserve. Mostly saturation and a mild “well-fed” warmth, not potion spam.
- Heirloom squashes. A few blocky gourd variants (butternut, hubbard, turban) grown like pumpkins, used as building blocks, seats, or carving blanks with different stencil sets. Shape matters more than jokes.
- Cider press. Apples plus a simple wooden press yield cider. Drinkable, stackable, warm light-brown bottles for shelves. No drunkenness; just a harvest drink and a brewing ingredient for subtle warmth effects.
- Spice rack. Cinnamon bark, dried clove-like seed pods, and nutmeg from rare autumn trees. Used in pies, candles, and dyed wax. Scarce enough to feel like a foraging reward.

**Light, textiles, and the home**
- Beeswax candles in an autumn dye set. Tapers, pillars, and a low hearth candle that melts down over time and can be recast. Light temperature stays warm; no colored disco glow.
- Leaf-and-twig wreaths, swags, and garlands. Wall and fence decorations built from leaf litter plus twine. A carved pumpkin can sit in the center of a wreath as a quieter alternative to wearing it.
- Wool and linen in a restrained fall palette: rust, ochre, ink, cream, pine. Cloaks or lined coats that are mostly cosmetic, with a small bonus against the chill of rain or high biomes rather than armor power.
- Hearth stones. A compact fireplace block that burns applewood, dried leaves, or peat and gives a small radius of “settled” comfort (slower hunger drain while nearby, similar to a campfire’s mood without the silly particles).

**World and season**
- Leaf litter and windrows. Fallen-leaf carpet that builds up under autumn trees, can be raked into baskets, and composts into rich soil. Raking is a quiet chore, not a minigame.
- Dusk mist in valleys and orchards. Short-range fog and muffled footsteps after sunset, clearing by morning. Atmosphere only; no hidden mobs riding the fog.
- Orchard groves and cider sheds as rare structures. Overgrown apple rows, a press, empty jars, and a stencil book. Feels abandoned for the season, not haunted.
- Owls and late geese as ambient mobs. Owls perch and call at dusk; geese pass overhead in small lines. No combat role, no item drops beyond the occasional feather for quills and fletching.
- A harvest moon night. Once a season, the moon is larger and warmer, crop growth ticks a little faster, and carved pumpkins hold their light longer. No event mobs.

**Carving station extensions**
- Pumpkin aging. Fresh carvings are bright; after several nights the edges dry and the dye mutes unless sealed with wax or syrup glaze. Encourages upkeep without decay looking rotten.
- Stencil books and copper printing plates. Players copy a stencil by pressing a plate, then share plates. Keeps the system craft-focused.
- Display forms. A stand, windowsill block, and fence-post mount so carved pumpkins read as household objects, not only helmets. Wearing one can still work like vanilla, with the same vision limits.
- Seed-saving. Particularly well-carved or rare-colored pumpkins can be dried for seeds that bias the next crop toward that rind color. Breeding stays simple: color tendency, not stats.

**Materials**
- Applewood and peat as fuels with distinct burn times and hearth interactions.
- Pine resin for sealing jars, waterproofing cloaks, and a matte finish on carved faces.
- Ink from walnut husks for writing stencil notes and darkening wool, separate from vanilla black dye so it feels seasonal.

**What to leave out**
Skip animated scarecrows with personalities, trick-or-treat drops, costume sets, screaming blocks, graveyard loot, and any mob that exists to startle. If a feature needs a punchline or a fright to justify itself, it is outside the tone you want.

If you want a next pass, the strongest spine is sap, cider, wax, and leaf litter tied back into the carving station, so the new systems feed the feature you already have.
