The mod already has dyeing, stencil carving, lit pumpkins, and dispenser support, so most of these ideas build on those systems.

Tone I aimed for: real harvest and Samhain folklore (old Celtic festival marking the end of harvest), gothic atmosphere, and Minecraft's own eerie side (the Pale Garden,
soul fire, sculk). The goal is quiet unease, not rubber bats.                                                                                                             
Builds on what already exists
- Candle-lit pumpkins. Light a decorated pumpkin with 1–4 candles instead of a torch.  Light level goes up with candle count, candle color tints the glow, and it         
  flickers more softly than a torch. You can snuff it out with an empty hand. It       reuses the lit-pumpkin and dispenser code almost directly.
- Pumpkins that decay. Placed decorated pumpkins slowly slump through three or so      stages: fresh, soft, collapsing. Honeycomb waxes one to keep it as it is, like     
  copper. This fits the season and gives players a reason to care about their          displays.
- Wearable decorated pumpkins. Wear a dyed and carved pumpkin on your head like a      vanilla carved pumpkin, with the same enderman protection. Your carving becomes    
  your mask.                                                                         - More stencils as exploration rewards. Motifs with an older feel: moth, raven,      
  crescent moon, owl, bare tree, a Samhain-style knot, warden, sculk shrieker. Put     the rare ones in trial chambers, ancient cities, and Pale Garden structures. That  
  keeps "finding a stencil" a real discovery.                                        - Custom stencil design. Paint your own design on a pixel grid at the Carving        
  Station. It's a big feature, but it's the one players would talk about most.                                                                                            
  Harvest & folklore
- Ornamental gourds and white "ghost" pumpkins. New crops found as rare seeds. White   pumpkins make great bases for dyeing, and warty gourds work as small decorative    
  blocks.                                                                            - Turnip lanterns. Before pumpkins, people carved turnips (the Irish and Scottish    
  tradition). A small hanging carved-root lantern would be a nice nod to that history  and a good lighting option.
- Scarecrow. A posable figure built from a hay bale, sticks, and a (decorated)         pumpkin, that you can dress like an armor stand. It could do something too: stop   
  rabbits eating crops and keep crows away (next item).                              - Soul cakes. A food based on the real "souling" tradition, where spiced cakes were  
  given out on Hallowmas in exchange for prayers for the dead. Add mulled cider,       pumpkin soup, and roasted pumpkin seeds as small, grounded harvest foods.

Atmosphere & creatures

- Crows. A passive mob that perches on fences and scarecrows, pecks at crops, and scatters in a flock when you get close. Mostly atmosphere, with a light gameplay loop tied to the scarecrow.
- Will-o'-the-wisps. Faint lights in swamps and Pale Gardens at night. They drift away as you approach, and if you follow one it sometimes leads to a buried cache (a good place for rare stencils). This is my favorite on the list: pure folklore and truly eerie.
- Moths. Ambient particles that gather around lit pumpkins and lanterns at night.
- The Thin Night. A night tied to the moon phase (e.g., the new moon) rather than the calendar, so it happens year-round and can be turned off in config. During it: low ground mist, more crows, wisps more common, candles flicker harder. Vanilla already has an Oct 31 mob-pumpkin easter egg, and this could build on that.

Decoration

- Weathering gravestones. Headstones you can write on like a sign, which slowly gather moss the way copper oxidizes. Keep them decorative only, so they don't clash with grave/death-chest mods.
- Wrought-iron cemetery fencing and gates. Plus a memorial candle or small shrine that pairs with soul cakes.
- Bundled corn stalks and dried-flower bundles. Classic harvest-porch decor.

Where I'd start

1. Candle-lit pumpkins and pumpkin decay/waxing. Cheap to build, deepen the existing pumpkin system, and very on-tone.
2. More loot-only stencils. Mostly art plus loot-table work, and they give the carving system more to discover.
3. Will-o'-the-wisps. The strongest "signature" feature, and the first real step outside pumpkins.
