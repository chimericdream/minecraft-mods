This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the
one accent goes) → **the type system and the palette** (which face does which job, the five size steps, which colour is the ground and where the
accent goes) → **the hero blueprint** → **the section blueprints**. Under each blueprint below, "Adjusted for this page" lists where it disagrees.
In practice: take geometry from blueprints (columns, proportions, what sits where) and take everything else from above them. Map every size a
blueprint names onto one of your five type steps instead of adding a sixth. Use one button shape, one corner radius and one pair of page margins on
the whole page, whatever individual blueprints say. If a blueprint names a kind of typeface (a light serif, a heavy grotesk), use the dealt face.

## 1. The idea
The whole page is a board of tiles fitted edge to edge with narrow gaps, like a tidy desk seen from above. Each tile is one thing: the name, a statement, a photograph, a number, a link, a short list. There is no hero and no sequence of bands; the page is one continuous composition that the eye roams. It suits subjects with many small true things to show (a studio with work, writing, clients and news; a product with several distinct capabilities) and it replaces the generic bento by having real content in every tile and a strict system for sizes.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A 12-column grid with 8px gaps and 8–12px outer margins, filled completely, no empty cells. Tiles use only four sizes (for example 6×4, 6×2, 3×2, 3×4 in column × row units of about 90px). Every tile is a flat filled rectangle: ground colour one step from the page, or a palette colour, or a photograph covering it. One radius everywhere (0, or 6–10px). The first screen contains: a name tile (the name at display size, the largest type on the page) with the wordmark set large, a statement tile with the description at 28–40px, two or three image tiles and one action tile in the accent colour. Text inside tiles is top-left aligned with 20–24px padding and never centred. Below the first screen the board continues with the same sizes; the last row is the footer made of tiles.

## 3. Type — General Sans
A grotesk with slightly geometric bowls, set bold and very large against plain 16px text with nothing in between. Suits studios, type and product launches, and finance or tech brands that lead with the name.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=general-sans@400,500,700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **General Sans** — weight 700 · tracking -0.02em · leading 0.9 · sentence case; sizes jump straight from 16px text to 10vw and larger
- Text: **General Sans** — 400 for body and lists, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.45 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.0625rem, 1rem + 0.25vw, 1.1875rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
One grotesk in two weights. Inside tiles: a large size for single statements, a small size for lists. No uppercase labels in tile corners.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. Page ground, tile ground one step lighter or darker, and one or two palette colours on selected tiles. Photographs supply the rest.

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** First choice: **real pictures of the subject's world from the open collections**: its city and street in old postcards and photographs, an old map of the neighbourhood, its materials up close, its trade in engravings, museum objects of its kind (references/imagery.md §3). Download what you use into the project with `--get` and credit every one on the page (`--credits` prints the footer line). They show the world around the client, never the client's own room, dish, product, person or team. Second, for what no honest picture exists of: draw the subject's own things in code, large, in one flat graphic language and the palette's real colours (its tiles, its windows, its objects; references/imagery.md §8). Third: the dealt photographs, only where an honestly generic picture fits (the sea, the weather, a material), never one a visitor could take for the thing itself and never an unrelated scene. One treatment for all of them. If this direction is built on photographs, plan the set before building (four to six pictures of one family) and reuse them at different crops and scales instead of finding or drawing a new picture for every slot; keep drawings as `<symbol>` + `<use>` or generate them with a small script so the page stays light. List every found picture (with its source and licence), every dealt photograph and every drawing that stands in for photography in your report.

Photographs fill whole tiles with `object-fit: cover`; choose images that crop well to both wide and tall tiles.

**Floor (the inspector checks it):** at least four real pictures on the page, usually six to twelve; SVG drawings do not count. One moving picture where an honest clip exists: `node <skill>/scripts/assets.mjs --video "<query>"`, used as a muted loop with a poster frame. Whatever this direction says about pictures, it is carried out with real ones.

## 6. Hero — Sheet with a text cell
The whole viewport is a sheet of photographs: six columns with 12px gaps and 12px outer margins, each column a vertical stack of frames at natural ratios with square corners. Each column starts at a different vertical offset (0, -90, -40, -130, -20, -70px) so rows never line up and the top and bottom frames are cropped by the viewport. Every frame has a flat strip 28px tall fused to its bottom edge in a solid palette colour, carrying one line of 13px text (a title or a place); strip colours rotate through four values. There is no header. In columns 3-4, starting at 28% of the height, one cell of the sheet is not a photograph but a filled panel in the tone opposite to the ground, 410px tall, sitting on the grid like any other frame with the same gaps on all sides. Inside it, top-left aligned on 24px padding: the wordmark at 28px, one sentence at 20px on up to three lines, the single action as a filled rectangle, and three text links on one line at the panel's foot.

On a phone: The text panel spans the full width as the first block, about 40% of the screen height, with the wordmark at 7vw, the sentence at 17px and the action full width. Under it the sheet resumes in two columns with 8px gaps, the right column started 60px higher than the left, colour strips kept on every frame. The links sit in one row at the panel's foot.
Headline: The wordmark at 28px plus one sentence of up to 70 characters inside the panel; nothing is set at display size.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) instead.
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. showcase — One image across tiles (mosaic)
One photograph is cut across a board of tiles. A grid of six columns and four rows with 8px gaps spans the full width between 12px margins; every tile is square (aspect-ratio: 1). Each tile shows its own piece of the same image: set the photograph as every tile's background at background-size: 600% 400% and step background-position by column and row, so the picture reads whole through the gaps. Five of the twenty-four tiles are swapped for flat colour tiles from the palette, never two adjacent: one holds the heading (two to four words at 2.6vw, top-left, 16px padding), three hold one fact each at 18px, one holds the action. On hover a tile's piece scales to 108% inside its own clip. Choose a photograph with one clear subject, so the board is legible at a glance.
On a phone: Three columns by six rows with 6px gaps, the photograph re-cut at background-size 300% 600% from a portrait crop. Four colour tiles only; the heading tile spans two columns in the top row with the heading at 6vw. Facts drop to 15px.

### 2. list — Giant word, filled bar rows (stack)
Sized for a reading column 520–640px wide, or centred at that width on a full page. The heading is one word (menu, hours, prices) in tall condensed capitals, its `font-size` set in container units (around 46cqw for four letters, tuned per word) so the letters fill the column from edge to edge, leading 0.8. 20px below, a stack of full-width filled bars, each 56px tall with 6px gaps, in a flat tone one step from the ground, with square corners or a 6px radius. Each bar carries a category name at 16px in medium weight at the left with 18px padding and a plus sign at the right. A bar opens in place as a `details` element: it keeps its fill, and its panel lists items at 16px, name left and price right-aligned with tabular figures, 14px between items, 20px padding. Six to nine bars; the first is open by default.
On a phone: The column is the full width with 16px margins, so the one-word heading spans the screen. Bars are 54px tall with 6px gaps and open the same way. Item descriptions, if any, sit under the name at 14px in the soft ink.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display General Sans, text General Sans) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

### 3. about — Team on colour tiles (grid)
A one-line heading of two or three words at 28–36px sits top-left; nothing sits to its right. 40px below, a grid of portrait tiles: six columns with 12px gaps across the full content width for 12 or more people, four columns for 4–8. Every tile is a 4:5 photograph treated identically so mixed-quality headshots look like one set: `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(1.15)` with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` over a flat tint, the tints rotating through four palette colours in a fixed order. Square corners or one small radius. Under each tile, left-aligned to its edge with 10px of space: the name at 15px in full ink and the role at 13px in the soft ink, up to two lines. Rows are 36px apart. A leftover tile in the last row stays at the left; it is not centred. No hover lift and no social icons.
On a phone: Two columns with 8px gaps; tiles stay 4:5 with names at 14px beneath. For more than ten people, switch to three columns and show the name only. The heading sits above at 24px. Tints keep their rotation, so the grid reads as a checkerboard of colours.

### 4. footer — The mark, built large (band)
On a flat colour field, the brand closes the page as a construction rather than as set type. Across the full content width and about 34svh tall, the wordmark is built from blocks: each letter is knocked out of its own solid rectangle in a palette colour, the rectangles differ in height (60–100% of the band) and butt together with 4px gaps, bottoms on one line. Where the project has a simple geometric mark or motif, enlarge that instead, drawn as inline SVG from flat rectangles, circles and quarter-arcs with the same 4px gaps. Below, 56px lower: four columns of plain links at 15px across columns 1–8, and two addresses or contact lines in columns 9–12. A last row carries the copyright at the left and one small filled button at the right. Space and the block construction do all the separating.
On a phone: The block wordmark spans the full width at about 22svh tall; a long name breaks into two rows of blocks. Link columns go two across at 15px, contact lines beneath at full width, then copyright and the small button on one row.

## 8. Motion — Shutter image
The one hero or showcase image opens from an edge with a clip-path over about 1.1 seconds while the picture inside settles from 1.12 scale to rest. Use it once, on the image that matters most; later images simply are there. Recipe: 'Image shutter' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The fit. A full board with consistent gaps and varied tile contents is satisfying in the way a packed suitcase is.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Tiles are filled, never outlined, and never carry shadows. Do not put an icon and a two-line feature blurb in every tile; vary what tiles hold. Decide the phone layout as its own board: two columns, tiles re-ordered so the name and statement lead.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
