# Design brief: Chimericdream Minecraft Mods site

Brief for restyling the docs site generated by `scripts/site/` (on the `docs-site` branch). Written to be
handed to the `design-taste-frontend` skill. Items marked **[skill decides]** are deliberately left to the
skill's judgment; it should pick a default and say what it chose in its report.

## 1. What it is

The public home for 34 Fabric/NeoForge Minecraft mods by chimericdream, published at
`https://chimericdream.github.io/minecraft-mods`. Every page is generated from files already in the repo:
each mod's README, CHANGELOG, `ideas/` folder and in-game guide, plus a site-wide ideas roadmap and
combined changelog. Nothing is hand-edited, so the design must work for whatever Markdown arrives.

## 2. Audience, in priority order

1. **A player** arriving from CurseForge or Modrinth who wants to know what a mod does, see it, and
   install it.
2. **A modpack author** checking dependencies (Architectury, ChimericLib, optional Opus), loaders and
   supported Minecraft versions.
3. **A returning fan** following the roadmap and changelog.
4. **The maintainer**, who must be able to regenerate everything with one command.

## 3. What a visitor should do

- Home: find a mod in under ten seconds and open it.
- Mod page: understand the mod, see what it looks like, then go to a download or the guide.
- Roadmap and changelog: scan quickly.

## 4. Feeling

**Fantasy, broadly.** The author's own taste is fantasy, and related aesthetics are welcome: high fantasy,
storybook, cartographer's maps, alchemist's or tinkerer's workshop, steampunk, illuminated manuscripts.
It does not have to be dark fantasy. The skill may choose whichever of these gives the strongest single
idea, as long as it holds together.

The content is practical (furniture, storage, building blocks, small fixes), so the best idea probably
pairs a fantasy world with craft: a workshop in a mountain keep, a catalogue of wares, a bestiary of
blocks. Mod pages are catalogue entries; the guide is the book.

Avoid: Minecraft font everywhere, dirt or stone-texture backgrounds, creeper imagery, neon gamer gradients,
a generic SaaS page (hero, three icon cards, testimonial), and fantasy clichés such as stock dragons
in every section.

## 5. Brand

- **Mark:** the author's CurseForge/Modrinth avatar, a crimson dragon draped across twin snow-capped
  mountains under a crescent moon, flat vector style on black. Placeholder trace:
  `docs/design/logo-placeholder.svg`. The author may supply the original SVG later, so reference it by
  path and don't bake it into CSS. **[skill decides]** Wordmark: the name is "chimericdream" and "chimeric" means a
  creature made of mixed parts, which the dragon and the mod collection both echo.
- **Palette seed**, sampled from the avatar and the README badges: near-black `#000`, deep violet
  `#2d1050` (ChimericLib badge `#2f0e48`), shadow violet `#1d0c31`, snow `#eceff1`, moon grey `#e2e2e4`,
  crimson `#8b1a1a` (Opus badge `#7a1f2b`). Treat this as the starting palette. The skill should keep it
  recognizable but may shift values to meet contrast and to build a proper light theme.
- **Type:** **[skill decides]**. Pick a distinctive pair that suits the chosen fantasy flavor, loadable from Google Fonts
  or self-hosted. Body text must stay highly readable at length.

## 6. Light and dark

Both are first-class and follow the OS setting by default, with an optional manual toggle that remembers
the choice. Dark is not "light inverted": design each with its own ground, ink and accent. The avatar's
black, violet and crimson suit dark naturally; the light theme needs its own ground (a pale snow or
parchment tone, not stark white) and the same crimson accent adjusted to meet contrast.

## 7. Imagery that exists

- Item and block textures from each mod's resources (pixel art; render crisp with
  `image-rendering: pixelated`).
- Per-mod icons where present; otherwise a generated tile (the current site uses a colored letter).
- In-game screenshots: producible with the `mc-marketing-screenshots` skill. **[skill decides]** Assume few or no
  screenshots exist; the design must look finished without them, and should leave room to add them later.
- Recipe data rendered as crafting grids.
- No stock photography and no invented imagery.

## 8. Content the design must handle

- A directory of 34 mods with status (beta/unreleased), version, and loader badges. There are no flagship
  mods, but four were chosen to show the range of what the author builds. Feature them on the home page
  and list the other 30 densely, rather than 34 equal cards:
  - **Minekea:** decorative and functional furniture, storage and building blocks (the largest mod).
  - **Hopper X-Treme:** a practical upgrade to a vanilla block.
  - **Effective Gear:** small quality-of-life tweaks to armor, weapons and tools.
  - **Camel Nostrils:** deliberately absurd ("This mod is weird. Really weird.").
  Together they span big and small, practical and silly; the featured treatment should show that range
  and the author's sense of humor, not present them as a uniform row.
- A mod landing page rendering the README (badges, feature lists, screenshots).
- Long-form reading: guide pages with callouts (tip, warning, important, danger), tables, code, recipe
  grids and item references.
- Changelogs (long, repetitive).
- An ideas roadmap table with status, plus per-idea and per-brainstorm pages.

The home and mod pages are "pages" in the skill's sense. Guide, changelog and roadmap are reading surfaces
and should favor typography, measure and navigation over spectacle.

## 9. Constraints

- **Keep the stack:** Bun + `marked`, static HTML out, no framework migration. Design lives in
  `scripts/site/` (`layout.ts` holds the CSS and shell). Change generator code freely; don't change what
  content gets published.
- **All links relative**, so the site works under the `/minecraft-mods/` base path.
- **Don't touch** the local-only `/local/` dashboard (`scripts/site/local/`), except that it shares the
  theme color tokens in `THEME_CSS`, so keep that export working.
- Fast: minimal JavaScript. Usable at phone width. Accessible: real contrast, visible focus, semantic HTML,
  keyboard navigation, reduced-motion respected.
- Everything must still work with the current sidebar navigation model (a per-mod tree of README,
  changelog, guide and ideas).

## 10. Success

- A screenshot of the home page reads as a hand-designed indie site, not documentation tooling.
- A mod page makes you want to install the mod.
- Reading a guide page is a better experience than the in-game book.
- Light and dark each look intended.

## 11. Out of scope

Search backend, accounts, comments, new content, and CurseForge/Modrinth listing art. **[skill decides]** A
client-side search box is welcome only if it is cheap and needs no new infrastructure.

## 12. How to run it

Treat this as a restyle/redesign of an existing site per the skill's special-case workflow: record the
"before" screenshots first, keep the stack and content, deal a direction using `--keep palette` (and
`type` if the author later fixes fonts), then prove the result with before/after screenshots at several
widths in both themes.
